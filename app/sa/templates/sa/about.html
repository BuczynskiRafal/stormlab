Stormwater Analysis System

Decision support for storm sewer design: hydraulic simulation, verification against design criteria and a graph neural network that suggests an action for every conduit.

  • EPA SWMM 5.2
  • 7 design checks
  • 9 recommended actions
  • 12 catchment categories

Overview

The application reads a SWMM model of a gravity storm sewer network, runs the simulation and assesses each conduit on two levels. Locally, it checks the conduit against hydraulic and geometric design criteria. At network level, a graph neural network looks at the conduit together with the conduits that drain into it and returns a probability for each of nine possible actions.

The system was developed in the doctoral research of Rafał Buczyński. It points to the conduits that need attention and to the kind of intervention worth considering. The decision, the sizing and the check by re-simulation stay with the designer.

Quick tour

  1. Open Analysis, pick a frost zone and choose Use test file. It loads the model of the Rakowiec estate in Wrocław (62 conduits, 37 subcatchments).
  2. On the Map tab, switch between passed checks and filling to see where the network is overloaded.
  3. Browse the Conduits, Nodes and Subcatchments tables, or export them to CSV.
  4. Select a conduit to open its panel: longitudinal profile, the seven checks, the recommended action and the probabilities behind it.
  5. Return to earlier runs from History, or upload your own .inp file (flow units: CMS).

How it works

  1. Simulation. SWMM runs on a temporary copy of the uploaded file, with the options declared in that file.
  2. Hydraulic verification. Every conduit is checked against seven design criteria.
  3. Catchment categories. Land-cover categories are read from the [TAGS] section; subcatchments without a tag are classified by a neural network.
  4. Recommendations. The graph neural network returns a probability distribution over nine actions for every conduit.

Recommendations

The network is represented as a directed graph in which every conduit is a node and edges follow the flow towards the outfall. Each conduit is described by 66 features: geometry, hydraulic state from the simulation, the catchment and rainfall above it, and the design requirements. Two GraphSAGE layers aggregate information from the inflow side, reaching four links upstream. The application serves the mean of five training runs of this model.

The output is a probability for each action rather than a single verdict. Several actions with similar probability are alternatives to weigh, not steps to carry out together.

Catchment categories

Twelve land-cover categories describe the subcatchments. When a subcatchment has no tag in the model, a small neural network assigns the category from twelve inputs: seven catchment parameters and five runoff results of the simulation.

arable, forests, marshes, meadows, mountains_rocky, mountains_vegetated, rural, suburban_highly_impervious, suburban_weakly_impervious, urban_highly_impervious, urban_moderately_impervious, urban_weakly_impervious

Evaluation

The recommendation model was trained and evaluated on a labelled dataset of 5,056 storm sewer network models with 107,248 conduits, simulated with the dynamic wave method. The data were divided by network family into training, validation and test parts (162, 53 and 51 families), so related networks never appear in more than one part.

Case study: Rakowiec, Wrocław

Limitations

  • A recommendation indicates grounds for intervention in the analysed state. It does not prove that the action is feasible or effective; that requires a new simulation of the modified network.
  • The model suggests the kind of action, not its size, such as a tank volume or a new diameter.
  • Each action refers to one conduit. Measures designed jointly for a sequence of conduits are outside the nine actions.
  • The model was trained on dynamic wave results. Files that declare another routing method are analysed as declared, and recommendations for them are less reliable.
  • Treat with caution the elements rare in the training data: conduits without a catchment above them, outfalls with a fixed water level, multi-barrel conduits. Pumping and infiltration are the least reliable actions.
